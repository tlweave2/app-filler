# Cover Letter Base

Reusable skeleton. The bracketed parts are the only parts that change per
application — everything else is yours once you write it.

Keep it to four short paragraphs. Nobody reads a full page.

---

{DATE}

Dear {HIRING_MANAGER_NAME or "Hiring Team"},

**Paragraph 1 — the hook (2 sentences).**
I'm applying for {ROLE} at {COMPANY}. {ONE SENTENCE THAT PROVES YOU KNOW
SOMETHING REAL ABOUT THEM — a product decision, something they published, the
problem this team owns. Not "I admire your commitment to innovation."}

**Paragraph 2 — the match (3-4 sentences).**
{The posting's top requirement, and your strongest evidence against it. Lead
with a number. This paragraph is the whole letter — if a reader stops after
it, they should already know why you'd be good at this job.}

**Paragraph 3 — the second match (2-3 sentences).**
{The posting's second requirement, same treatment. If the role is a stretch in
some dimension, this is where you address it directly and briefly — a reader
who spots a gap you didn't mention assumes you're hiding it.}

**Paragraph 4 — the close (2 sentences).**
{What you want, plainly. No "I look forward to hearing from you at your
earliest convenience."}

Sincerely,
{YOUR_NAME}

---

## Notes to self

- Address it to a person when you can find one. A named manager beats "Hiring Team."
- Mirror the posting's vocabulary. If they say "platform reliability," don't
  write "uptime engineering" — some ATS keyword filters are literal.
- If the form makes the cover letter optional: write one when you're genuinely
  a strong match or the role is a stretch, skip it on volume applications.
  A generic letter is worse than no letter.
